## Article 1 — Purpose and Scope

This Circular sets out the minimum requirements for Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) that all Licensed Financial Institutions (LFIs) under the supervision of the Central Bank of the UAE must implement.

For the purposes of this Circular, "Licensed Financial Institutions" includes banks, exchange houses, finance companies, payment service providers, and any other entity licensed by the CBUAE.

## Article 2 — Risk-Based Approach

2.1 All LFIs shall conduct an institution-wide Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (ML/TF) risk assessment at least annually. The risk assessment shall consider:
- Customer risk factors (including geographic risk, industry risk, and transaction patterns)
- Product and service risk factors
- Delivery channel risk factors
- Country and geographic risk factors

2.2 The ML/TF risk assessment shall be approved by the Board of Directors or Senior Management and documented in writing.

## Article 3 — Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

3.1 LFIs shall perform Customer Due Diligence before:
- Establishing a business relationship
- Carrying out occasional transactions above AED 55,000 (or equivalent in foreign currency)
- When there is a suspicion of ML/TF regardless of transaction amount
- When there are doubts about the veracity of previously obtained identification data

3.2 CDD measures shall include:
- Verifying the customer's identity using reliable, independent source documents
- Identifying the beneficial owner and taking reasonable measures to verify their identity
- Understanding and documenting the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship
- Conducting ongoing due diligence on the business relationship

3.3 Where CDD cannot be satisfactorily completed, the LFI shall not open the account, commence a business relationship, or perform the transaction. The LFI shall consider filing a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR).

## Article 4 — Suspicious Activity Reporting

4.1 LFIs shall file Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) with the UAE Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) when there is a suspicion or reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are the proceeds of a criminal activity or are related to terrorist financing.

4.2 SARs shall be filed promptly and no later than 24 hours after the suspicion is formed.

4.3 Failure to file a SAR or filing a materially incomplete SAR shall be subject to administrative penalties of up to AED 5,000,000 per violation.

4.4 LFIs and their employees shall not disclose to the customer or any third party that a SAR has been or will be filed (tipping-off prohibition).

## Article 5 — Record Keeping

5.1 LFIs shall maintain all CDD records, transaction records, and correspondence for a minimum of five (5) years after the termination of the business relationship.

5.2 Records shall be maintained in a manner that allows timely retrieval by the CBUAE or the FIU upon request.

## Article 6 — Compliance Function

6.1 Every LFI shall appoint a dedicated Compliance Officer at the senior management level who is responsible for the AML/CFT program.

6.2 The Compliance Officer shall have direct reporting access to the Board of Directors and shall not hold concurrent operational responsibilities that could create a conflict of interest.

6.3 LFIs shall provide adequate resources, including staffing and technology systems, to the AML/CFT compliance function.

## Article 7 — Training

7.1 LFIs shall conduct AML/CFT training for all relevant employees at least annually.

7.2 Training shall cover:
- ML/TF risks specific to the institution and its products
- CDD procedures and red flag indicators
- SAR filing procedures
- Sanctions compliance requirements
- Consequences of non-compliance for both the institution and individuals

## Article 8 — Penalties

8.1 Violations of this Circular may result in administrative penalties including:
- Fines of up to AED 10,000,000 per violation
- Suspension or revocation of the LFI's license
- Public censure
- Referral to criminal prosecution authorities

8.2 The CBUAE may impose additional supervisory measures including enhanced monitoring, restrictions on activities, or appointment of an external monitor.
